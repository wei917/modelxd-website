# Captions

Write in the user's language, and in their voice if their message gave you a
sample of it. Match their register — if they wrote to you casually, do not
hand back a press release.

## Shape

Open with the single most surprising concrete fact, not a preamble. "A 25
year old turned $225M into $20B and lost it in one morning" earns the next
line; "Here's an interesting story about the markets" does not.

Then the mechanism in plain words — the part a reader could repeat to someone
else. Then the consequence. Close on the thing that is still unresolved,
because that is what makes people reply.

## Rules

- No hashtag walls. One or none.
- No "thread 🧵" unless it is actually a thread.
- Numbers beat adjectives. "Down 54%" beats "collapsed dramatically".
- Never state as fact something the source only implies. If the user's own
  draft asserts a causal link the evidence does not carry, keep the fact and
  mark the inference as theirs: "I don't know if this was designed or
  coincidence" is stronger than an accusation, and it cannot be refuted.
- Fit the platform's limit from PLATFORMS.md. If the content genuinely needs
  more room than the platform allows, say so and offer to split it rather
  than silently truncating the idea.

## Real people and claims

If the post names a real person or company and makes a negative claim,
separate what is documented from what is inferred, in the caption itself.
This is not hedging — it is what makes the post survive being quote-tweeted
by someone hostile.
